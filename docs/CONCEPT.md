# Concept

## Prototype question

Can incomplete weather readings, limited resources, persistent network planning, and consequential warnings create an enjoyable 25–35 minute first-week loop?

The player operates a small weather bureau serving three inhabited floating-island districts. The simulation is fictional but consistent: each hazard has a learnable three-part evidence pattern, and every result explains the actual pattern so failure is attributable to a decision rather than an arbitrary answer.

First-time players receive an optional guided bureau tour after the Day One briefing. It follows the live daily flow, focuses one interface area at a time, and requires real district, phase, hazard, and warning-district interactions without choosing answers or spending resources on the player's behalf. Completion can be skipped and replayed.

An interrupted first week autosaves locally after completed actions and phase changes. The next launch offers a choice to resume the exact desk state or replace it with a new week; completed weeks do not remain as resumable history.

## Daily loop

Briefing → inspect local readings → decide whether to spend on remote observations → classify the hazard and severity → choose districts → confirm → resolve → understand the calculation → review the next-day outlook and optionally maintain the network.

The core tension is confidence versus cost and time. Day One teaches a complete pattern, then its overnight outlook introduces construction on the persistent fixed-site sensor/relay graph. Day Two withholds one essential reading that can be collected from the prepared network. Day Three adds plausible faulty evidence and makes a second observation late. Before Day Four, High Ridge fails during maintenance, forcing a repair, alternate-route, or deferral choice. Day Five turns the accumulated network into route-dependent options for a severe final forecast.

## Network planning

The bureau begins with HQ, one High Ridge relay, and an Industrial electrical sensor. Fixed field sites can hold a relay and one specialized sensor. Healthy relays create transmission paths; an Industrial relay can reroute both Farm Spire and Harbor traffic when High Ridge is offline. After Days One through Four, a broad outlook supports one optional budget-funded installation or repair. During the following forecast, connected-reading collection and surveys compete for limited daily capacity and warning time. Equipment planning and evidence gathering therefore remain linked without charging both against the same action pool.

## Fictional hazards

- Sparkstorm: rising charge, falling pressure, eastward clouds; electrical and communications damage.
- Glasswind: rapidly changing wind direction, high crystal readings, low humidity; crop and exposed-relay damage.
- Cloudburst: high moisture, slow dense clouds, rising condensation; flooding and harbor disruption.

## District identities

- Farmland: most vulnerable to Glasswind and benefits from preparation time.
- Industrial: most vulnerable to Sparkstorms and has stronger flood protection.
- Harbor: most vulnerable to Cloudbursts and suffers from late preparation.

No individual citizens are simulated. District state exists only at the level needed to calculate vulnerability, warning usefulness, damage, trust, and budget.
