# Concept

## Prototype question

Can incomplete weather readings, limited resources, persistent network planning, and consequential warnings create an enjoyable 25–35 minute first-week loop?

The player operates a small weather bureau serving three inhabited floating-island districts. The simulation is fictional but consistent: each hazard has a learnable three-part evidence pattern, and every result explains the actual pattern so failure is attributable to a decision rather than an arbitrary answer.

First-time players receive an optional guided bureau tour after the Day One briefing. It follows the live daily flow, focuses one interface area at a time, and requires real district, phase, hazard, and warning-district interactions without choosing answers or spending resources on the player's behalf. Completion can be skipped and replayed.

## Daily loop

Briefing → inspect local readings → decide whether to spend on remote observations → classify the hazard and severity → choose districts → confirm → resolve → understand the calculation.

The core tension is confidence versus cost and time. Day One teaches a complete pattern. Day Two withholds one essential reading and introduces construction on the persistent fixed-site sensor/relay graph. Day Three adds plausible faulty evidence and makes a second network action late. Day Four opens with High Ridge disabled, forcing a repair, alternate-route, or evidence-only choice. Day Five turns the accumulated network into route-dependent options for a severe final forecast.

## Network planning

The bureau begins with HQ, one High Ridge relay, and an Industrial electrical sensor. Fixed field sites can hold a relay and one specialized sensor. Healthy relays create transmission paths; an Industrial relay can reroute both Farm Spire and Harbor traffic when High Ridge is offline. Installation, repair, collection, and surveys compete for the same limited daily capacity, so infrastructure and evidence gathering are part of one decision rather than a separate economic minigame.

## Fictional hazards

- Sparkstorm: rising charge, falling pressure, eastward clouds; electrical and communications damage.
- Glasswind: rapidly changing wind direction, high crystal readings, low humidity; crop and exposed-relay damage.
- Cloudburst: high moisture, slow dense clouds, rising condensation; flooding and harbor disruption.

## District identities

- Farmland: most vulnerable to Glasswind and benefits from preparation time.
- Industrial: most vulnerable to Sparkstorms and has stronger flood protection.
- Harbor: most vulnerable to Cloudbursts and suffers from late preparation.

No individual citizens are simulated. District state exists only at the level needed to calculate vulnerability, warning usefulness, damage, trust, and budget.
